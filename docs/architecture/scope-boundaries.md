## In Scope

AgentLogistics covers commercial logistics work where goods are received,
stored, moved, controlled, fulfilled, transported, returned, measured, or
improved.

In-scope work includes:

- logistics operating model analysis;
- inbound receiving and dock workflow;
- storage and warehousing operations;
- inventory control and reconciliation;
- replenishment, picking, packing, staging, and shipping;
- material-handling analysis;
- transportation and freight planning;
- warehouse design and capacity reasoning;
- logistics systems and data analysis;
- logistics KPIs and continuous improvement;
- warehouse labor and operating planning;
- returns and reverse logistics;
- logistics safety planning and hazard identification;
- jurisdiction-specific compliance research when isolated in a specialization.

## Out of Scope for the Universal Core

These areas may touch logistics but should not be treated as universal
AgentLogistics core skills:

- procurement sourcing strategy;
- supplier negotiation;
- manufacturing process engineering;
- product design engineering;
- structural engineering approval;
- equipment certification;
- operator certification or training signoff;
- legal compliance advice;
- customs brokerage advice presented as legal advice;
- tax, duty, or tariff advice presented as professional advice;
- HR policy, employment law, or labor relations;
- financial accounting beyond logistics cost analysis;
- consumer moving, personal storage, or household logistics unless adapted into
  a separate specialization.

## Boundary Definitions

### Logistics

Logistics is the operational planning, movement, storage, control, and
coordination of goods and related information through commercial flows.
AgentLogistics focuses on executable operational reasoning, not broad corporate
strategy alone.

### Supply-Chain Management

Supply-chain management is broader than logistics. It may include sourcing,
procurement, supplier strategy, manufacturing, demand planning, finance, and
customer strategy. AgentLogistics may support the logistics interface with these
functions but should not become a general supply-chain management repository.

### Procurement

Procurement decides what to buy, from whom, under what commercial terms, and
with what supplier strategy. Procurement is out of the universal core except
where procurement facts are required inputs for inbound logistics, inventory
policy, or transportation planning.

### Manufacturing

Manufacturing transforms materials into goods. AgentLogistics may cover
line-side material flow, staging, WIP movement, finished-goods handling, and
manufacturing logistics interfaces. It should not own process engineering,
tooling design, production recipes, or manufacturing quality systems unless a
future specialization is explicitly created.

### Commercial Storage

Commercial storage is the controlled holding of goods. It may occur in
warehouses, yards, terminals, cold rooms, tanks, silos, or third-party storage
facilities. AgentLogistics may reason about storage requirements, capacity,
handling, status, rotation, and risk.

### Warehousing

Warehousing is a logistics facility discipline covering receipt, storage,
movement, replenishment, picking, packing, staging, shipping, returns,
inventory control, and supporting systems.

### Transportation

Transportation covers movement between nodes by parcel, courier, truckload,
LTL, rail, air, ocean, intermodal, drayage, and related modes. Universal
transportation skills may reason about planning, cost, utilization, and
performance. Mode-specific regulations and international rules must be isolated
by jurisdiction or specialization.

### Inventory

Inventory is the stock of goods tracked by identity, quantity, status, location,
ownership, availability, lot, serial, expiration, or condition. AgentLogistics
focuses on operational inventory control, not financial accounting standards.

### Material Handling

Material handling covers the physical movement, protection, storage, and control
of goods inside or near logistics facilities. AgentLogistics may compare
equipment classes and flow concepts, but must not certify equipment, operators,
or engineered installations.

## Safety and Compliance Boundary

AgentLogistics can help users ask better safety and compliance questions. It
cannot approve a facility, certify a process, train an operator, sign off a
rack system, determine legal compliance, or replace qualified professionals.

Safety-sensitive outputs should:

- identify assumptions;
- separate observations from inferences;
- state the jurisdiction when relevant;
- recommend authoritative source verification;
- use conservative uncertainty language;
- escalate to qualified review where risk is material.

## Specialization Boundary

The following belong outside the universal core until specialized source and
validation standards exist:

- Canada;
- United States;
- international logistics;
- dangerous goods;
- cold chain;
- food logistics;
- pharmaceuticals;
- automotive;
- ecommerce;
- manufacturing;
- retail distribution.

Specializations may depend on core skills, but core skills should not depend on
specializations.
