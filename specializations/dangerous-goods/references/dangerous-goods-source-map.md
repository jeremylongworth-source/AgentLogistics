# Dangerous Goods Source Map

Completion token: `AGENTLOGISTICS_AL_21_DANGEROUS_GOODS_READY`

Access date for the AL-21 source list: 2026-09-03.

In this source map, dangerous goods requirements are mode-specific and
jurisdiction-specific. This source map is a starting point for research and
qualified-review handoffs, not a complete legal register, classification
decision, packaging approval, training certificate, emergency response plan, or
compliance determination.

## Source Categories

- PHMSA hazardous materials transportation sources cover US hazardous materials
  rules, classifications, hazard communication, training, packaging,
  shipping papers, incident reporting, and carrier-facing research.
- eCFR Title 49 HMR sources provide current online text for US hazardous
  materials rules, including Parts 171 through 180 and mode-specific
  transportation requirements.
- Transport Canada TDG sources cover Canadian dangerous goods handling,
  offering for transport, transportation, means of containment, documentation,
  safety marks, training, emergency response, and cross-border research.
- Justice Laws TDG Regulations sources provide current Canadian regulatory text
  for dangerous goods transport research.
- UNECE Model Regulations sources provide international model regulation
  context for classification, listings, packaging, marks, labels, placards,
  documentation, and transport provisions. They are not a substitute for the
  governing jurisdiction or mode rule.
- ICAO Technical Instructions and IATA DGR sources cover air dangerous goods
  research, airline acceptance context, and air-mode constraints.
- IMO IMDG Code sources cover maritime dangerous goods packing, container,
  stowage, and segregation research.
- OSHA, EPA, local fire code, environmental regulator, carrier, port, terminal,
  airport, insurer, employer safety program, SDS, product specification, and
  manufacturer sources may control storage, workplace safety, waste,
  environmental, emergency response, or local operating constraints.

## Required Boundaries

- Do not state that a material, article, mixture, waste, package, mark, label,
  placard, shipping paper, SDS, facility, vehicle, container, trailer, route,
  mode choice, carrier, response action, training record, employee, process, or
  system is compliant, approved, certified, safe, legal, accepted, releasable,
  or ready for transport.
- Do not approve dangerous-goods classifications, hazardous-materials
  classifications, packing groups, UN numbers, proper shipping names, special
  provisions, packaging designs, compatibility groups, segregation tables,
  quantity limits, limited quantity or excepted quantity status, emergency
  response actions, cleanup, disposal, carrier acceptance, air or vessel
  acceptance, customs declarations, environmental determinations, personnel
  qualifications, training certificates, or live system changes.
- Use source dates, jurisdiction labels, mode labels, and product or hazard
  labels. If current official sources are unavailable, return an evidence
  request or draft research brief.
- Keep user evidence, official sources, assumptions, source conflicts,
  observations, inferences, operational next steps, and qualified-review needs
  separate.
