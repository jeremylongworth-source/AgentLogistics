# International Logistics Source Map

Completion token: `AGENTLOGISTICS_AL_22_INTERNATIONAL_LOGISTICS_READY`

Access date for the AL-22 source list: 2026-09-03.

In this source map, international logistics requirements are lane-specific and
jurisdiction-specific. This source map is a starting point for research and
qualified-review handoffs, not a complete legal register, customs entry,
export filing, sanctions screening, export-control classification, duty
calculation, Incoterms contract interpretation, carrier acceptance, port
release, terminal release, or freight forwarder approval.

## Source Categories

- ICC Incoterms sources cover current Incoterms rule version context and trade
  term research. The full ICC rules are copyrighted and must not be reproduced.
- CBP import and eCFR invoice sources cover US importing, commercial invoice,
  packing list, entry-document, and importer-responsibility research.
- CBSA import and export sources cover Canadian commercial import, export,
  account, release, and reporting research.
- WCO HS, origin, valuation, and SAFE Framework sources provide international
  customs classification, origin, valuation, and trade-facilitation context
  without replacing country-specific customs rules.
- US Census AES and Foreign Trade Regulations sources cover US Electronic
  Export Information and export filing research.
- BIS EAR and Commerce Control List sources cover US export-control
  classification and license-question research.
- OFAC sanctions sources cover US sanctions program screening and escalation
  research.
- FMC detention and demurrage sources cover US ocean shipping demurrage and
  detention invoice and dispute research.
- IMO FAL sources cover maritime arrival, stay, departure, ship-port interface,
  and maritime single-window research.
- ITA common export document sources provide official US trade-document
  education for commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, air
  waybills, certificates, and destination-specific document questions.
- Carrier, customs broker, freight forwarder, port, terminal, airline, ocean
  carrier, rail, drayage provider, insurer, customer, bank, product regulator,
  origin authority, destination authority, and user-provided contracts or SOPs
  remain required evidence for operational decisions.

## Required Boundaries

- Do not state that a shipment, customs entry, export filing, AES filing,
  sanctions screen, ECCN, HS classification, country of origin, customs value,
  duty rate, tax amount, Incoterms interpretation, commercial invoice, packing
  list, bill of lading, air waybill, certificate, container, drayage move,
  port release, terminal release, carrier booking, customs broker action,
  freight forwarder action, or system change is compliant, approved, accepted,
  released, legal, complete, or financially authorized.
- Do not approve customs entries, customs release, export filings, sanctions
  determinations, restricted-party decisions, export-control classifications,
  license requirement determinations, duties, taxes, Incoterms contract terms,
  trade-document content, bill of lading issuance, carrier acceptance, freight
  forwarder acceptance, port or terminal release, demurrage or detention
  payment, customer commitments, financial postings, or live system changes.
- Use source dates, jurisdiction labels, lane labels, party-role labels, mode
  labels, and product labels. If current official sources are unavailable,
  return an evidence request or draft research brief.
- Keep user evidence, official sources, assumptions, source conflicts,
  observations, inferences, operational next steps, and qualified-review needs
  separate.
